case study 01 — lindström eShop

Making a catalog behave like a store

Redesigning Lindström's B2B eShop configurator for mats and industrial wipes inside an engine that could not support a shopping basket.

Role Lead UX/UI Designer
Users B2B buyers + prospects
Team PO, dev, brand
Shipped Configurator v2
original configurator full screen preview

The situation

Lindström's eShop is where facility managers order mats and industrial wipes for their sites. It carried the whole service relationship, but the interface read as an internal tool: grayscale panels, no fixed navigation, body copy sized for a document rather than a product, and an order summary that ate half the screen with no way to collapse it.

"Friction at the exact moment a customer was committing to spend."

People missed options, lost the "Next" button, and could not tell whether their order had actually been placed.

The constraint that defined the project

The platform's backend engine could not support a shopping basket. No cart, no line-item editing, no checkout flow. Technically, the eShop was a catalog. Commercially, customers were treating it as a store.

Rather than argue for a rebuild we would not get, I reframed the brief: design the confidence of a transactional store on top of a catalog.

order summary locked open and hidden carousel issues

What I found

A heuristic evaluation of the live configurator, prioritised by how directly each issue blocked an order:

01. Hidden Carousel: Oversized type pushed the product carousel below the fold; users never saw options that appeared after a selection.

02. Order Summary: Occupied half the viewport permanently, with no way to collapse it while choosing products.

03. Lost "Next" Button: On the final step, the side-mounted navigation scrolled entirely out of view.

04. "Add Service" Dead-end: Read two ways—confirming the order vs adding a product. With no basket, the wrong reading cost a sale.

proof-of-concept - fixed header + brand colour integration

Three bets

I translated the findings into three strategic bets and used them to arbitrate scope with the product owner:

Treat B2B users like customers: People buying mats for a warehouse shop online in their own lives. Approachable, branded, familiar beats "enterprise software".

Rebuild the hierarchy, not the engine: Scale type down, fix the navigation, give the summary a hide state—recover screen space instead of waiting on new platform features.

Let copy do the work code can't: Where the basket was missing, language had to carry the mental model.

What shipped

Six changes, all fully feasible within the existing engine framework:

01. Show/hide order details: Collapsed while choosing, expanded to review. Reclaims half the screen at the moment it matters.

02. Fixed global navigation: Persistent header on scroll, plus one FAQ section merging separate FAQs.

03. Type scaled down: Selection, carousel, and description now sit in one clean view—no scrolling to discover changes.

04. Visible order count: The number of services configured, always on screen—the closest thing to a basket the engine allowed.

05. "Would you like another mat?": Ambiguity removed without writing a single line of new backend logic.

06. "Next" moved to bottom: Ends the step where the step ends, matching every checkout users already know.

final interface - order details expanded view

Outcome

Shipped inside the existing engine with no platform work and zero delay. The eShop now reads as Lindström rather than an unbranded tool.
