A site with everything and nothing findable

Oatland Island has animals, trails and school programs worth visiting for. The website buried all of it. Two thirds of visitors to sites like this leave after a single page — and it is not because they lost interest.

Heuristic review said the cause was one level up: the navigation itself.

• A second navigation header that quietly sent people to an external website.

• No call to action on the landing page. Nowhere to buy a ticket or book a visit.

• Dated layout with no hierarchy, so scrolling never felt like it would lead anywhere.

• Redundant categories everywhere. The as-is sitemap was too wide to read on one screen.

What we learned:

Education is the center's real value, and its best kept secret. Visitors did not know the programs existed.

There are two audiences, not one. Families planning a Saturday, and teachers planning a field trip. The site spoke to neither.

People said the website was fine, then struggled on it. We designed for the behaviour, not the interview.

On site, the day is about the animals. The site should lead with the experience.

"The parents who said the site was fine were the same ones who couldn't find the event page."

Five decisions the research made for us

1. "Merge the duplicate categories and drop the second navigation."

2. "Give Education its own top-level home, so schools can see it."

3. "Anything bookable gets a visible call to action."

4. "Event info or tickets in three clicks or fewer."

5. "Warmer copy and real animal photography instead of stock filler."

Formative round: right direction, fix the details

• The new global navigation worked, and people said so first.

• The friendlier copy and simpler layout landed with both audiences.

• Dropdown contrast was too low — reworked for readability.

• Imagery needed to be stronger — swapped in better animal photography.

What I took away

"The structure work paid better than any visual choice we made. Rewriting the IA around card sorting, adding the CTAs and cleaning up the SEO are the things that moved 27 seconds to 9 — and those are business numbers as much as usability ones."

It also taught me to trust observed behaviour over reported comfort: the parents who said the site was fine were the same ones who could not find the event page.
