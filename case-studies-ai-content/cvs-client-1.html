Redesigning retail for trust and efficiency

CVS Health challenged our team to design a retail experience that reduces external theft while keeping the customer experience positive, efficient, and profitable.

Role Experience Lead
Team 18 designers
Client CVS Health
Duration 10 weeks
team with CVS Health clients

The brief

This project's research findings and solutions are covered under NDA. This page shares the challenge, my role, and the team. Get in touch if you'd like to hear more about my process.

Over 10 weeks, our 18-person cross-functional team developed proposals addressing one of retail's hardest design problems: how do you design against theft without designing against the customer?

"Designing against theft without designing against the customer."

My role

I served as Experience Lead — responsible for the end-to-end experience strategy across the team's proposals. That meant synthesizing research into a direction, making sure our concepts held together as an experience rather than a collection of ideas, and representing the user perspective in every stakeholder conversation.

Beyond the deliverables, my job was to keep 18 people pointed at the same problem.

The challenge

Retail theft solutions tend to create friction for everyone — locked cases, receipt checks, surveillance signage. The design challenge was finding interventions that work without punishing the 99% of customers who aren't stealing.

That tension — between security and experience — was the brief. Everything we designed had to hold up against both.

The team

18 designers, researchers, and strategists. 10 weeks. One brief.

18-person cross-functional SCADpro team

SCADpro brings together multidisciplinary student teams to work on real client briefs. Our team spanned UX, service design, research, brand, and communication design.
