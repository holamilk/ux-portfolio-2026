Acclio: Settle In, Stress Less

Designing for belonging, not just bureaucracy

Solo Passion Project · UX Design · Service Design · Mobile App

Acclio hi-fi screens
Paperwork was never the hard part. Belonging was.

Overview

Problem
Government tools help immigrants with documentation. Nobody helps them with the rest — understanding cultural norms, finding community, navigating daily life in an unfamiliar place. The emotional side of settling in goes completely unaddressed.
Solution
Acclio is a culturally adaptive platform that helps newcomers navigate local services and ease the emotional stress of settling in — practical guidance, community connection, and personalized support in one place.

Why I built this

I moved from Mumbai to Helsinki. The cultural gap was enormous and I had no single place to go for help — not with the unwritten social rules, not with finding community, not with the loneliness that comes before you feel at home anywhere. I built Acclio because I needed it and it didn't exist.

This project focuses on research, concept development, service design thinking, and user journey design rather than high-fidelity UI alone. I wanted to explore viability, scalability, and long-term impact — not just ship screens.

What the research said

I started with secondary research — government reports, academic studies, migration stress literature — before running 50+ surveys and 4 contextual interviews to validate and pressure-test what I found.

feel overwhelmed accessing basic services in their first year

report moderate to severe homesickness
87%
named making friends their hardest challenge

Five things only the interviews told me:

  • Trust comes first. People fear being scammed or overcharged by unfamiliar services. They want community-verified recommendations and privacy by default.
  • The unwritten rules matter more than the written ones. Nobody wants to be the person who got the sauna etiquette wrong.
  • Humans beat interfaces. Generic advice frustrates people. They want guidance shaped to their background, language, and life stage.
  • Small unknowns cost real time. Over-preparing for appointments, guessing at appliances, skipping food because the label is unreadable.
  • People expect to outgrow it. They treat Acclio as a phase tool — which means the information has to stay verified and current while they use it.

The question every settlement app has to answer

Settling in is temporary. If the app only serves week one, it gets deleted by month three. So I mapped the full user journey and asked what a user three months into a new city still opens it for — and what keeps them a year in.

The value has to move with the user: from surviving, to socializing, to belonging.

What I built

Home tab

Quick links to essentials, a personalized three-stage settlement plan, local and national alerts, and an AR camera that explains unfamiliar objects and cultural equivalents.

Community tab

Local events, interest-based groups, a Q&A forum, and a Buddy system for connecting with locals or fellow immigrants individually or in groups.

Discover tab

Personalized local recommendations based on onboarding preferences, plus a directory of essentials and favorites nearby.

Learn tab

Tips, guides, and language lessons that help newcomers navigate daily life with more confidence over time.

A note on trust and safety

Trust was a core theme in my research. Newcomers are in a vulnerable position — they don't know yet who or what to rely on. I designed with that in mind throughout.

The Buddy system uses badge verification so users know who they're connecting with. The Q&A forum is moderated. Onboarding only collects what directly improves the experience — nothing more. Data transparency is built in so users can see what's stored and delete it. Safety isn't a feature. It's the foundation the whole product sits on.

What testing told me

Users responded well to personalization, the AR feature, and the onboarding flow. The thing they asked for that I hadn't built yet: a community Q&A forum. They wanted to ask questions and get answers from real people — not just browse curated content. I built it. When they tested it again, they said it was exactly what they had pictured.

What I'd do differently

The trust infrastructure needs more design depth — verification, moderation, and safety flows for the Buddy system especially. The AR feature is genuinely useful but probably the wrong MVP priority given its technical complexity. And I designed the first 90 days really well. The transition out — when users start to outgrow the app — deserved more work.
