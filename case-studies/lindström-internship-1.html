Lindström eShop Redesign

Making a catalog behave like a store

Redesigning Lindström's B2B eShop configurator for mats and industrial wipes inside an engine that could not support a shopping basket.

Experience Design Intern · Lindström Group · 3 months

full bleed hero image - redesigned eShop UI
Lindström's eShop worked. It just wasn't easy to use.

Overview

Problem
Navigation was unintuitive, copy was ambiguous, and the visual design didn't reflect the brand, making it harder than it needed to be for B2B clients to configure and order services.
Solution
A focused redesign of the configurator flow, collapsible UI, rewritten copy, fixed navigation, and brand color integration, all designed to work within the platform's existing technical constraints.

About the project

Lindström is a B2B textile services company operating in 20+ countries. Their eShop let clients configure and order services like industrial mats and wipes. Navigation was unintuitive, the visual design didn't reflect the brand, and ambiguous copy was creating confusion at key decision points. I owned the redesign from audit to stakeholder sign-off.

There was one major constraint from the start. The platform ran on a backend with no native shopping basket, so it functioned more like a catalog with a configurator than a real store. I wasn't rebuilding it. I was making it work better as it was.

Four things stood out in the audit

I started with a heuristic evaluation to find the biggest friction points before talking to anyone.

  • Text size pushed key content out of view. When a user made a selection, a contextual carousel appeared below, but body text was large enough that users had to scroll to see it. Most didn't know it was there.
  • The order summary took over the screen. During active configuration it expanded to fill half the viewport, leaving almost no space to browse the remaining options.
  • The Next button disappeared at the wrong moment. At the end of the configuration flow, the primary action button scrolled out of view. The global nav did the same thing on scroll.
  • "Add service" meant different things to different people. Some users read it as finalizing their order. Others thought it meant adding a different product category. It was doing neither.

What I tried before landing on what to ship

I explored a lot before narrowing down. Fixed global headers, brand color integration, new ways to present product images, side by side layouts, modal popups, and restructured hierarchy throughout.

I put together proof of concepts rather than wireframes because getting stakeholder alignment on constrained design requires showing the reasoning, not just the output. Some ideas weren't feasible given technical limitations, so I focused on high-impact changes that improved usability without requiring a rebuild.

What this project taught me

The redesign wasn't a full overhaul and it wasn't meant to be. The projected outcomes are higher configurator completion, less confusion at key decision points, and a visual experience that feels like Lindström.

I practiced identifying which change would deliver the highest usability return inside fixed constraints, then making that case clearly enough for stakeholder approval.

The most useful skill here wasn't ideation. It was choosing the most valuable change inside real constraints and earning alignment around it.
