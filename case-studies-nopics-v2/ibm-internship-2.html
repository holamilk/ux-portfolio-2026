IBM Z Mainframe Security

Designing to mitigate security vulnerabilities and accelerate future design workflows through AI

UX Design Intern · IBM · Summer 2026 · Enterprise Security

Overview

Problem
IBM acquired a threat detection and response product for IBM Z mainframe environments and GA'd zSecure Detection by combining 5 standalone products under one name before they were actually unified. The recently acquired product ran on outdated components with security vulnerabilities.
What I did
  1. Migrated the acquired product into IBM's Carbon design system and shipped it to customers.

The situation

IBM GA'd zSecure Detection by grouping 5 standalone security products under one name. They weren't actually unified. One of them, a threat detection and response product for IBM Z mainframe environments, had just been acquired and was running on outdated components that introduced security vulnerabilities into customer environments.

I joined as UX Design Intern on the zSD team, working in Carbon Design System v11 alongside the design team.

Part One — Carbonizing Threat Monitor

Before design started there was a business decision to make. Ship the Carbonized screens early in a split release, or hold and ship the full product in Q4. Splitting would have hit a closer deadline but fractured the experience right as customers were adjusting to the new platform.

I pushed for shipping it whole. One quarter later, customers got a coherent product.

split vs full release timeline diagram

This wasn't a component swap. Before touching a single screen, I used object-oriented UX to map the product's structure — core objects, attributes, and relationships — and asked how this acquisition fit into the broader portfolio. That systemic foundation let me simplify the UI, improve interactions, and make critical threat response features accessible within a dense security environment.

Part Two — A side quest that became something bigger

After Threat Monitor shipped I kept thinking about how tight the timeline was. The design ramp alone, understanding an acquired product deeply enough to redesign it well, took weeks. With four more products to unify, that wasn't going away.

I started talking to designers across IBM, IMS, z/OS, Infrastructure, Research, and Security, asking how they were working. Most teams were using AI for ideation at best. Nobody had built workflows specific to design delivery. I saw a gap and built two workflows to close it.

Workflow 1 — Product Analyser

Takes an acquired product's technical docs and legacy UI screenshots. Outputs a product summary, an object model, and Carbon v11 layout previews in one pass.

Workflow 2 — Consistency Auditor

Audits screens against a baseline from another product in the portfolio. Checks terminology, spacing, fonts, color tokens, and detached variables. Outputs a severity-triaged report with screen overlays and Dev Mode annotations.

The first time I ran it on Threat Monitor, the deviations it caught were implemented the same day. It is now named in the official zSD unification proposal and in active use by another designer on the team.

Impact

  • Threat Monitor Carbonized and shipping — security vulnerabilities removed, screens in active development.
  • ~70% reduction in ramp time — 1.5 to 2 weeks reduced to 3 to 4 days.
  • Consistency Auditor adopted same-day on first run, now named in the official zSD unification proposal.
  • Reusable components and patterns built for all of zSecure.
  • Co-facilitated unification workshops that aligned architecture and requirements across design and engineering.

What I took from this

Shipping Threat Monitor whole instead of early was the right call. Building tools so future teams don't face the same choice was the follow-through.
