Overview

The work
Rachael Thrash — educator and author of Let Learners Lead — built Co-Create Schools to help schools shift from top-down culture mandates to student-teacher partnerships. She needed a visual identity, book illustrations, and a website to launch it.
What I delivered
Complete brand identity and style guide, illustrations for the published book, and a full end-to-end website — live at cocreateschools.com.

The brand

Co-Create Schools sits at an interesting intersection — it needs to resonate with school administrators making institutional decisions and with teachers looking for practical tools they can use Monday morning. The brand had to feel credible to both without feeling corporate to either.

Navy for authority and trust. Soft yellow and cream for warmth and approachability. Every choice was made to reflect an organization that believes schools work better when students are partners, not recipients.

The book illustrations

I created the visual framework illustration for Let Learners Lead — mapping the four phases of the Co-Create process. The people who would actually use these illustrations are students in group activities and teachers facilitating them.

That meant the visuals had to be clear enough for a classroom, engaging enough for a student, and professional enough to sit inside a published book.

The website

Teachers are the primary visitor. They come with specific questions — does this actually work, what does the framework look like in practice, how do I bring this to my school. I structured the site around surfacing those answers quickly while giving Rachael the space to communicate her methodology and make the case for why student-led culture change works.

The tension between what a visitor needs immediately and what a client wants to say is a design problem in itself. I wrote and refined the copy for every section to resolve that — leading with the visitor's question, earning the deeper explanation.

The outcome

The site launched alongside the book publication. Co-Create Schools went from a placeholder domain to a live consultancy with a brand, a product, and a place to send people — built and shipped in under six weeks.
