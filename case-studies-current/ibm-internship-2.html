IBM zSecure Detection: Designing Inside an Acquisition

IBM Z Mainframe Security

Designing to mitigate security vulnerabilities and accelerate future design workflows through AI

UX Design Intern · IBM · Summer 2026 · Enterprise Security

A product shipped before it was unified.

Overview

Problem
IBM acquired a threat detection and response product for IBM Z mainframe environments and GA'd zSecure Detection by combining 5 standalone products under one name before they were actually unified. The recently acquired product ran on outdated components with security vulnerabilities.
What I did
  1. Migrated the acquired product into IBM's Carbon design system and shipped it to customers.
  2. Co-facilitated design thinking & engineering workshops to align on a shared goal and action plan.
  3. Built 2 AI workflows now being used in the org.

Part One — Carbonizing Threat Detection Product

Before design started there was a business decision to make. Ship the Carbonized screens early in a split release, or hold and ship the full product in Q4. Splitting would have hit a closer deadline but fractured the experience right as customers were adjusting to the new platform.

We pushed for shipping it whole. One quarter later, customers got a coherent product.

Time crunch meant unconventional route

I had no time to conduct research, complete competitive benchmarking, or even create low-fidelity wireframes. We jumped straight into design.

This wasn't a component swap. Before touching a single screen, I used object-oriented UX to map the product's structure — core objects, attributes, and relationships — and asked how this acquisition fit into the broader portfolio. That systemic foundation let me simplify the UI, improve interactions, and make critical threat response features accessible within a dense security environment.

Ultimate stakeholder collaboration

Since we couldn't delve into traditional UX processes, this project demanded collaboration and communication more than ever. We spent several weeks in stakeholder meetings to understand how the product worked and how best to design for it.

Working with Carbon Design System

Going in, I assumed Carbon would make things straightforward — the components are there, you use them. What I didn't anticipate was how much judgment still goes into it. Design systems create consistency, but it's on the designer to adapt patterns to the actual product context. For this product, that meant applying enterprise components to dense security data that cybersecurity professionals need to read and act on fast. The system gives you the building blocks. What you do with them is still entirely yours.

Working with our amazing devs

Collaborating with our developer team and scoping out release plans with them was a very fruitful process. I learned a lot about best practices for handoffs and presenting designs to any kind of audience, thanks to my lead, Julia.

Final redesigns that shipped

After weeks of work, the design is in development and shipping in Q4. This was a shared product, but I stepped in as an individual contributor and took full ownership of significant chunks end to end — from design decisions to developer handoff — and I am really proud of that accomplishment.

Gap closed: This tool helps automate consistency auditing processes across vast portfolios that can be a very manual and slow process. Using this tool can help achieve alignment faster and start taking action.

Part Two — An AI side quest that became something bigger

After Threat Monitor shipped I kept thinking about how tight the timeline was. The design ramp alone, understanding an acquired product deeply enough to redesign it well, took weeks. With four more products to unify, that wasn't going away.

I started talking to designers across other product teams and orgs, asking how they were working. Most teams were using AI for ideation at best. Nobody had built workflows specific to design delivery. I saw a gap and built two workflows to close it.

Workflow 1 — Product Analyser

Takes an acquired product's technical docs and legacy UI screenshots. Outputs a product summary, an object model, and Carbon v11 layout previews in one pass.

Gap closed: This tool helps accelerate the initial stages of understanding and defining a product. It also gives designers a head start on creating components and patterns, the building blocks of any product. Entering the design stage with that foundation can help teams stay on track for quarterly releases.

Workflow 2 — Consistency Auditor

Audits screens against a baseline from another product in the portfolio. Checks discrepancies that are not easily caught by the eye: terminology, spacing, fonts, color tokens, and detached variables. Outputs a severity-triaged report that also includes solutions on how to rectify the inconsistency, with screen overlays to point to the problem area and Dev Mode annotations.

The first time I ran it on my project, the deviations it caught were implemented the same day. It is now named in an official project proposal and in active use by another designer on the team.

Impact

  • Accelerated the design workflow 2x, helping Threat Monitor ship within the planned release window
  • Built reusable components and patterns on the Carbon design system, now available for other IBM security product teams
  • Co-facilitated unification workshops that produced a shared architecture plan and aligned requirements across design and engineering
  • Built AI tools that automate repetitive design tasks and free teams to focus on the decisions that actually require human judgment
