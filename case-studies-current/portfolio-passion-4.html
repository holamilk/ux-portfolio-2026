Website and Brand Design

My Portfolio

A never-ending pursuit

Solo Passion Project · Necessary to get a job · Duration: 2 days

They say treat your portfolio like a UX project. So I did. Here's the case study.

A small confession before we get into it.

I spent all summer unable to work on this. Then two days before I needed it, I built the whole thing. Two days — and honestly my best version yet. Though every designer loves their portfolio in the moment and hates it later, so check back with me on that.

Glimpse into the emotional stages I went through

Method

I wanted to code parts of my website but was hesitant. Little did I know that I would end up vibe coding the entirety of it. Turns out the thing I was most afraid of was also the thing that got me unstuck. Every interaction, piece of content, layout and feature was articulated by me down to the smallest detail.

I put in my rough notes, previous case studies, and design vision and made sure not a single judgment was left to AI. I am the designer. AI was the developer.

Primary Research

30+ people. Years of feedback. It never felt right.

Recruiters, managers, coworkers, teachers, professors, designers, peers, parents. I sought feedback obsessively and my portfolio still never felt done (grateful for all the amazing advice). This raises the ultimate UXer question: why is it that I can move efficiently through client work and tight deadlines, but the moment I sit down to design for myself I get completely stumped? Not sure still.

Turns out the hardest client I've ever had is myself.

Secondary Research

I bookmarked every portfolio I admired under "cool folios." And I never thought I would say this, but I think I did TOO MUCH competitive analysis in hindsight. Everyone had something distinct. A motif. A specific color. A signature motion. I didn't have that and it led to decision fatigue. I was convinced I needed to find it before I could move forward.

This bookmark folder once opened, is a never ending list

Finding a brand identity

Dilemma

I kept getting stuck on the brand. The colors. The typography. The animations. Hours in Framer, Figma, Readymag going down rabbit holes that looked cool but served nothing.

The portfolio graveyard

My ex-portfolios — it's not you, it's me. Truly.

2023–2024

2024–2026

Getting clarity

Sure, the landing page matters — recruiters spend 6 seconds deciding whether to keep going. But I had it backwards. The WORK is the point. Everything else exists to get someone there faster.

Once that clicked (while I was trying to sleep), everything else followed.

Design

The landing page should lead with the work. Nothing else.

If the work is the star, the portfolio is the stage. That's where the confetti idea came from — a spotlight, a celebration, work front and center the moment you land.

I then shifted my focus to clearly structuring my case studies. I moved away from cookie cutter formats to writing each one around the actual goals of the project, the challenges and workarounds, the decisions I made and why, and the business impact or projected outcomes those decisions drove.

Once that was in place, then I had fun with my brand: cute little animations, seamless micro-interactions, copywriting, colors.

Testing

This is the part where you come in.

There are portfolios I visited years ago that I still remember — not because of the work alone, but because the experience of moving through them left me with something. That's what I'm going for. A site that leaves you with a smile. So get in touch and tell me what you think!

I'll know it's working when it makes someone smile.

Also if I'm employed after graduating, that would be a great KPI to measure the success of my product.

What I found

My style and brand isn't a motif. It's not a motif or signature animation. It's intentionality, care and a little bit of humor — making sure that anyone who comes here has a great time navigating it. That's a principle I can carry into every version of my work and I hope it shows.

What I'd do differently

Close the "cool folios" tabs sooner. The competitive analysis helped me understand what I respond to as a viewer but I stayed in research mode long past the point where it was serving me.

Also what I'd do differently is change the entire portfolio. I love it now but come back next year — I can say there's a 95% chance of that happening.
