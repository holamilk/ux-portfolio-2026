SCADpro × CVS Health

Rethinking the CVS In-Store Experience

Designing for comfort and convenience while reducing theft

Experience Lead · SCADpro × CVS Health · 10 weeks

How do you design against theft without designing against the customer?

Overview

Brief
CVS Health wanted to make their stores a place of comfort and convenience — reducing shrinkage while improving customer satisfaction and store profitability. The challenge: most theft prevention creates friction for everyone, not just the people stealing.
What we did
Developed a retail experience strategy that addressed theft reduction and customer experience as one problem, not two. Our solutions were later presented at the RetailSpaces industry conference in Texas alongside CVS representatives.

The challenge

Retail theft solutions tend to punish everyone. Locked cases, receipt checks, surveillance signage — they create friction for the 99% of customers who are not stealing. CVS needed something different: interventions that reduce shrinkage without eroding the experience that keeps customers coming back.

Every concept had to hold up against both — security and experience.

How we approached it

We started by understanding why theft happens and what actually deters it — not just physically, but psychologically. Theft is enabled by anonymity, opportunity, and the belief that nobody is watching. We looked at what creates hesitation at the moment of decision, not just detection after the fact.

We also mapped the honest customer's journey. Where does security signage create anxiety? Where does friction slow people down? Where does the store feel unwelcoming before anyone has done anything wrong? The two lenses — deterrence and experience — revealed the same pressure points.

Defining our success metrics

Since there were so many users and criterias to consider, we needed a tool that can help us determine whether our solution advocates for all sides in the story, the colleagues, the customers, and the mitigation of theft.

Reaching a solution

We mapped ideas across every tier that touches the in-store experience — from the store floor itself to the digital and community layers that shape how customers arrive and what they expect when they get there. We called this approach the "ripple effect".

My role

I was Experience Lead — responsible for the end-to-end experience strategy across the team's proposals.

That meant synthesizing research into a direction, making sure our concepts held together as a coherent experience rather than a collection of individual ideas, and keeping the user perspective central in every stakeholder conversation. With 18 people across multiple workstreams, my job was also making sure everyone was pointed at the same problem.

From classroom to conference

After presenting our solutions to CVS Health as a student team, we were invited to present alongside CVS representatives at the RetailSpaces industry conference in Texas — where professionals from companies across the retail industry gathered to share where the field is headed.

That arc — from a student brief to an industry stage — was one of the most formative experiences of my time at SCAD.
