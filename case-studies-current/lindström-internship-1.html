Lindström eShop Redesign

Lindström eShop: From Confusion to Conversion

Building a B2B configurator to sell more than one service at a time

Experience Design Intern · Lindström Group · 3 months

Lindström's eShop worked. It just wasn't easy to use.

Original UI

Overview

Problem
Lindström's eShop let B2B clients configure and order services like industrial mats and wipes — but the platform was built for one service at a time, had no shopping basket, and didn't reflect the brand. Clients were leaving without discovering the full value of what Lindström offers.
What I did
Delivered short-term usability fixes to the existing configurator and designed a long-term need mapping tool that asks clients about their situation, then surfaces a personalized bundle of services — turning a catalog into a consultative commerce experience.

Reimagined e-shop

About the project

Lindström is a B2B textile services company operating in 20+ countries. Their eShop let clients configure and order services like industrial mats, wipes and other services. The current platform was lacking intuitive navigation and quick checkout flows which led to higher user drop-off.

So the project was divided into two distinct tracks.

Short-term

Immediate, low-disruption fixes to make the existing configurator usable for current customers. Collapsible UI, rewritten copy, fixed navigation, and brand color integration, all designed to work within the platform's existing technical constraints.

Long-term

A more ambitious redesign addressing the platform's fundamental limitation: no shopping basket. Rather than working around it, the long-term vision reimagines the configurator as the core commerce experience — surfacing multi-service bundle options personalized to each user's situation and making it the primary way customers discover and purchase services.

This case study focuses on the long-term redesign.

The short-term solution addressed the first point directly — collapsible order summary, fixed nav, rescaled text, repositioned buttons, rewritten copy.

Short term design solution based on heuristic evaluations

But the structural problem remained.The long-term opportunity was to change the purchasing model entirely — rather than configuring services one at a time, we support multi-service purchases in a single flow.

How might we enable customers to discover, configure, and purchase multiple Lindström services in one seamless experience — personalized to their actual needs?

A new approach

The existing configurator asked users to make product decisions before understanding their own problem. Pick a mat. Choose a size. Select a color. But most clients don't come in knowing they need a mat — they come in knowing they have dirty floors and high cleaning costs.

Ask about the problem first. Let the service recommendation follow from that.

Need mapping works in three phases.

01

Current Setup

A screener to understand the client's existing situation.

02

Identifying Needs

Exploring operational pain points, recurring challenges, and long-term goals.

03

Tailored Solutions

Suggesting service packages based on understanding, not guesses.

The line of questioning shifts from services to needs.

Most sales conversations start with "what are you looking for?" Need mapping reframes that entirely. The objective is to understand what the client is trying to solve — not what they think they want to rent.

Don't ask

  • "What type of mat would you like?"
  • "Which hygiene service do you want?"
  • "Would you like to add another service?"

Do ask

  • "Where are dirt and moisture creating the most work?"
  • "Which cleaning tasks cost your teams the most time?"
  • "What other facility challenges are connected to this problem?"
"From what we understand, you are having trouble with XYZ, so we think these particular services will directly address those problems."

Proof of concept — long term

The goal was to give clients full autonomy. Whether they know exactly what they want, are browsing for a single service, or have no idea where to start — the platform should meet them where they are and guide them from there.

Three entry points, one platform:

Don't know what you need

The need mapping configurator asks five questions about your business and surfaces a personalized service bundle. No sales call required to get to a recommendation.

Know what you want

Browse all services directly and start an order in one click.

Buying in bulk or managing products

A full product catalog with search, filter, and account management built in.

Need mapping configurator — find the right service bundle in under 2 minutes.

What this project taught me

The short-term redesign taught me how to identify the highest-value change inside fixed constraints and make that case clearly enough for stakeholder approval.

The long-term work taught me something different — that usability fixes alone can't expand what a platform is capable of. Sometimes the constraint isn't technical. It's the question you're asking users to answer.
